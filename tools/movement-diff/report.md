## How close is acacia-physics to BDS?

acacia-physics (`97ec4c7c`, 2026-11-11) matches BDS 1.26.51 bit for bit in **41 of 59**
scenarios. unastar matches 54 and bedsim 46. acacia and bedsim agree in 37. The 18 that differ
fall into these groups:

| group | scenarios (first differing tick) | acacia vs bedsim | evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| A diagonal move vector is not normalised | `flat_strafe_diagonal`, `flat_sprint_strafe`, `flat_sneak_diagonal`, `wall_corner_diagonal` (t6), `wall_walk_into` (t26), `ledge_sneak` (t46) | **regression**: bedsim matches BDS here | t6 of `flat_strafe_diagonal`: BDS moves 0.0692965 per axis (0.98 × 0.1 / √2), acacia 0.0707107 (the unnormalised 0.98 vector, clamped) |
| The sprint ends when the sprint key goes up | `flat_sprint` (t36), `sprint_jump` (t46): the ticks the key is released while forward is still held | regression | `flat_sprint` t36: BDS keeps the sprint while forward is held (0.13 × 0.98 acceleration); acacia walks (0.1) |

slime-bounce rule (the impact speed after gravity over the distance fallen) before unastar did.

### acacia's claims, checked one by one

1. **"A port of bedsim that keeps its f32 operation order."** Mostly true: in the 37 scenarios
   where acacia and bedsim agree, they agree bit for bit. Three of its departures make it worse
   than bedsim against BDS: the move vector, the sprint's end and the tiny-velocity cut-off.
   None of these is listed among its deliberate deviations.
2. **"The walk acceleration rotates the impulse with libm sinf/cosf, in BDS's order."**
   Confirmed. `flat_odd_yaw_walk` (yaw 37.5) and `flat_turning_walk` match BDS bit for bit;
   bedsim, which uses a different sine, does not. unastar arrived at the same rule from BDS.
3. **"The sine table is Linux BDS's (glibc sinf)."** Not testable as stated, and wrong for the
   BDS measured here. Our BDS is the Windows build. At yaw 0, a glide's look vector picks up a
   horizontal residue from the table:
   - BDS: x velocity 1.691715e-8 at tick 12 of `elytra_glide`;
   - acacia: 3.6959906e-9;
   - bedsim: -5.2e-18.

   acacia's own README says Windows BDS fills the table with MSVC's `sinf`. A Linux table does
   not reproduce Windows BDS. Its glide still diverges six ticks later than the others.
4. **"Very close to BDS."** 41 of 59 scenarios bit for bit, with diverging trajectories in the
   other 18, against 46 for bedsim, the code it ports. Eleven of its 18 failures are its own
   departures from bedsim (groups 1 to 3 above). Its strengths are its own fixes: the yaw
   trigonometry, the slime bounce, scaffolding and soul sand.

**Verdict:** acacia-physics is not closer to BDS than bedsim, the code it ports (41 vs 46 of 59).
It is behind unastar (54). Its deliberate fixes (the walk acceleration, the slime bounce) are
right. Three undocumented departures (the diagonal move vector, the sprint's end, the tiny
velocity) cost it more scenarios than its fixes gain. The sine-table claim targets Linux BDS
and does not hold on the Windows BDS 1.26.51 measured here. Its claim of being "very close"
rests on a diff harness against bedsim only, and the comparison with BDS does not support it.
